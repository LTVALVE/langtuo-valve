Ball Valve vs Butterfly Valve: Which One Should You Choose?

📅 2026-10-29 👥 朗拓阀门技术团队 🔖 Maintenance · Checklist

Buyers often ask us which valve is better: ball or butterfly. The honest answer is that neither is universally better — the two designs solve different problems. A ball valve is built for tight shut-off; a butterfly valve is built for space-saving flow control. This guide compares them on the criteria that matter for real projects.

What a Ball Valve Does

A ball valve uses a rotating ball with a bore through its centre. When the bore aligns with the pipe, the valve is open; turn the ball 90 degrees and the bore blocks the flow, giving bubble-tight shut-off. The quarter-turn operation is fast and the design is compact, which is why ball valves dominate isolation duty in process, water and energy lines.

What a Butterfly Valve Does

A butterfly valve uses a disc on a shaft, rotating inside the pipe bore. The slim disc and short face-to-face dimension make butterfly valves much lighter and cheaper in large sizes. They are the standard choice for modulating service — throttling flow with a positioner — and for large-diameter lines where a ball valve would be heavy and expensive.

Key Differences: Isolation vs Flow Control

  • Shut-off quality — ball valves give bubble-tight isolation with a soft or metal seat; butterfly valves give tight shut-off with soft seats but can weep slightly in throttled positions with metal seats.
  • Flow control — butterfly valves modulate flow smoothly across a wide opening range; ball valves are poor throttling devices, especially at small openings where erosion is severe.
  • Pressure drop — a full-bore ball valve has nearly zero pressure drop; a butterfly valve has a small drop even fully open because the disc stays in the flow path.
  • Size and weight — butterfly valves win at DN200 and above: shorter, lighter and typically 20–40% cheaper at the same rating.
  • Operating torque — butterfly discs need lower torque to actuate, so pneumatic or electric actuation costs less at large sizes.
  • Cavitation and erosion — ball valves should not be used for throttling; butterfly valves should not be held at very small openings for long periods.

When to Choose Which

Choose a ball valve when you need positive shut-off: isolation before a pump, tank outlet, gas or steam lines, and any service where a leak in the closed position is unacceptable. Choose a butterfly valve for modulating control with a positioner, for large water or HVAC lines, and for slurry or air services where a compact, low-torque valve saves space and money.
