Knife Gate Valve: When and Where to Use It

📅 2026-10-27 👥 朗拓阀门技术团队 🔖 Valve Selection · Gate Valve

Standard gate valves clog and jam in media that contains solids. That is exactly the service a knife gate valve is built for: its sharp-edged gate cuts through sludge, pulp and slurry and seals against solids-laden flow. If your line handles anything thicker than clean water, this valve type deserves a close look.

How a Knife Gate Valve Works

A knife gate valve uses a thin, sharpened gate that slides vertically between two seats, slicing through the media as it closes. Because the gate travels fully out of the body when open, there is no pocket where solids can pack and block the valve. The design is simple, and replacement of the gate and seats is fast and inexpensive compared with repairing a standard gate valve.

Knife Gate Valve vs Standard Gate Valve

  • Gate shape — the knife gate has a sharp cutting edge; a standard gate has a wedge or slab that pushes media aside.
  • Body design — knife gates have a slim, lightweight body with a straight-through bore; standard gates are bulky and can trap solids in the body cavity.
  • Sealing — knife gates seal on the gate edge against replaceable seats; standard gates seal on wedge surfaces.
  • Solids handling — knife gates cut through solids; standard gates jam on them.

Typical Applications

  • Sludge lines in wastewater treatment plants
  • Pulp and paper stock lines
  • Mining and mineral slurry handling
  • Dry bulk solids and pneumatic conveying systems
  • Fly ash and dust collection systems

Materials and Seat Options

The gate is typically stainless steel (304 or 316) for its cutting edge and corrosion resistance, with a body in cast iron, ductile iron or cast steel. Seats can be metal-to-metal for abrasive and high-temperature service, or resilient (rubber, EPDM, PTFE) for bubble-tight shut-off in sludge and water lines. For heavily abrasive slurries, hard-facing the gate edge extends service life.

Actuation

Knife gate valves operate with a lever or handwheel in small sizes, but most are actuated: pneumatic cylinders give the fast, powerful stroke that cutting through sludge requires, and electric actuators suit remote and automated plants. In slurry service, specify the cylinder size with the same care as the valve — insufficient force is a common cause of gate stalls.

Selection Tips

  • State the solids content, particle size and abrasiveness of the media
  • Confirm the shut-off requirement: tight (resilient seat) or solids-tolerant (metal seat)
  • Choose the gate material to match corrosion and wear
  • Select actuation force with a margin for sticky media
  • Check the bore: full bore avoids bridging and flow restriction
